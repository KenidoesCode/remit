# Personal brand: TECHUILAGUY

**Pranauv Shrinaath S.** — the friendly neighbourhood developer.

## The positioning

Someone who looks like the approachable guy in the room, and then turns out to
have built something absurdly serious. The reviewer's arc is:

> "this is a nice interface" → "wait, that's a real policy engine" →
> "who built this?" → "oh, *that's* the vibe" → "I want to talk to him."

The identity is the setup. The engineering is the punchline.

## Voice

Lowercase where it's conversational. Precise where it's technical. Never both in
the same sentence.

**Sounds like him:**
- "buy running shoes under ₹5000. it may not spend ₹5001."
- "go on. try to break it."
- "here's everything i broke building this."
- "following someone else's blueprint isn't really my thing."

**Does not sound like him, and is banned:**
revolutionising, next-generation, seamless, game-changing, unlocking,
disrupting, cutting-edge, empower, leverage, "in today's fast-paced world",
any sentence 500 other AI startups could also say.

**The test:** if a line could appear on any other Buildathon submission, delete
it.

## The four beliefs the reviewer must end up holding

1. **He can engineer.** — 80 tests, a pure policy engine, an evaluation harness.
2. **He can think.** — intent-to-transaction drift is an original framing, not a
   feature list.
3. **He can lead.** — the system is explained the way a lead explains it to
   another engineer: clearly, without condescension, without hiding the parts
   that don't work.
4. **He'd be fun to work with.** — the humour, the "try to break it" invitation,
   the honesty about failures.

Every section of the experience should be checkable against which of the four it
serves. A section serving none of them is decoration.

## The closing posture

Confident, not grateful. No "thank you for your time". No "please consider me".

The final line is an invitation between peers, not a request from a candidate.
