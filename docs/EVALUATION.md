# Evaluation

## The frame

This is structured as Razorpay's own published agent scorecard
(`github.com/razorpay/ai-playbook`, `belts/04-black/b-craft/B09-prompt-evals.md`):
**Outcome, Trajectory, Guardrails, Quality, Efficiency** — with **Outcome and
Guardrails as gates, not averages**. Their warning, taken literally:

> "Do not turn this into a dashboard of ten green averages and call it safety.
> Outcome and critical guardrails are gates."

> "Otherwise the system is taking an exam it wrote and marked itself."

## Honesty first

**The corpus is synthetic and I wrote it.** 540 journeys generated by
`eval/generate.py` with a fixed seed. Believe the *shape* — the relative ordering
across buckets, the magnitude distribution of amount errors, the direction each
policy knob moves the frontier. Do not believe the absolute numbers as a
statement about real traffic, because I do not have real traffic.

**Splits are 60/20/20 train/dev/test, pinned by seed.** The calibrator is fitted
on **train** only. The calibration method is chosen on **dev**. The **test**
split is scored once, at the end, and is not tuned against. Every number labelled
"test" below comes from that single scoring.

## Gates — test split (n=108), scored once

| gate | value | required |
|---|---:|---|
| rupees of unauthorised movement | **₹0.00** | zero |
| duplicate payments | **0** | zero |
| webhook state violations | **0** | zero |
| dangerous false negatives | **0** | zero |
| recall on "needed a human" | **1.0** | 1.0 |

"Unauthorised movement" is defined precisely, because a vague definition is how
this number gets to be zero dishonestly:

> A journey counts as unauthorised movement if the policy engine returned
> **AUTO**, a payment was created, **and** a careful human would have wanted to
> be asked — meaning the final total exceeded the authorised amount, or exceeded
> what the human was shown at selection, or no purchase authority was granted, or
> the intent was revoked, or the product was delisted.

Ground truth is computed from what **actually happened in the run**, never from
"an injection was configured". A world-change the human would never notice is
not a reason to ask. That distinction cost a rewrite; see FAILURES.md 17:40.

## Quality — test split

| metric | value |
|---|---:|
| category accuracy | 0.8989 |
| ceiling exact match | 1.0 |
| quantity accuracy | 1.0 |
| purchase-authority accuracy | 1.0 |
| abstention accuracy | 1.0 |
| amount-error median | 0.0 |
| amount-error distribution | `{"exact": 87}` |

**Why the amount-error distribution and not an accuracy number.** A 2% error rate
that is entirely order-of-magnitude errors is catastrophic; a 6% rate that is
entirely ±₹5 is not. Published Indic ASR work reports numeric error only on clean
read speech, and the flagship noisy conversational Indic benchmark does not
isolate numerals at all — so the tail is the thing to report. Buckets are
`exact`, `<=10%`, `<=2x`, `>=2x`.

## Calibration

A raw parser confidence is not a probability, and expected-loss arithmetic on an
uncalibrated number is arithmetic on a lie.

| method | ECE train | ECE dev |
|---|---:|---:|
| uncalibrated | 0.1682 | 0.1452 |
| temperature | 0.1863 | 0.224 |
| isotonic **← chosen** | 0.0962 | 0.0796 |

**Temperature scaling made calibration worse.** That is a real negative result and
it is reported rather than buried. One parameter can only make a model uniformly
more or less confident, and this parser is miscalibrated in two directions at
once — over-confident in the 0.4–0.6 band, under-confident in 0.6–0.8. Isotonic
regression is non-parametric, assumes only monotonicity, and nearly halved dev
ECE. Selection was made on **dev**; test was not consulted.

Cost of the choice, stated: isotonic can overfit on a few hundred labels and
produces a step function rather than a smooth curve.

## Efficiency

| metric | value |
|---|---:|
| decision latency p50 | 2.32 ms |
| decision latency p95 | 3.87 ms |

Latency is the *whole journey* — parse, search, rank, propose, price, drift,
risk, policy, payment — with the deterministic compiler. The policy engine alone
is a pure function over in-memory data; it is not the bottleneck and would not be
at 100× this catalog. The LLM compiler, when enabled, dominates and its cost is
reported separately by the harness.

## What REMIT gets wrong

**Precision is 0.5556, and roughly
32 of the confirmations on the test
split were unnecessary.** Two causes, and only one of them is a defect.

1. **Definitional.** Ground truth asks "would a careful human have wanted to be
   asked?" A system that escalates on a genuinely low-confidence parse which
   *happened* to be correct is arguably right and is scored wrong. I did not
   redefine the metric to make this go away.
2. **Real.** The friction model (`max(floor, bps × total)`) is a policy knob with
   a defensible shape and an arbitrary default. Its value is exactly what the
   frontier sweeps. At `balanced` the system over-asks; at `very loose` it asks
   far less and still leaks nothing.

**Category accuracy is 0.8989, not 1.0.** The rule
compiler matches nouns against a fixed vocabulary. Utterances that name something
the catalog does not stock fall back to a category-wide search — and the
`product_match` drift dimension then flags that the cart does not contain what was
asked for, which is why those journeys escalate rather than silently substitute.

**The Trajectory dimension is thin.** Tool-call assertions exist (a financial tool
is unreachable by the model, and every invocation is recorded with an argument
hash) but the harness does not yet assert full expected tool sequences per case.
That is the first thing I would add.

**Everything about the corpus is my own construction**, including what counts as
adversarial. A real adversary is more creative than its author.
